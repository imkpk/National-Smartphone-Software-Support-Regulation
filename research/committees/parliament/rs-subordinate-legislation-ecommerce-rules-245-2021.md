# Parliamentary Report Note — Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020

## 1. Report title [FACT]

**Two Hundred Forty Fifth Report** on **The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020**.

## 2. Committee / Commission [FACT]

**Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Rajya Sabha** (2019–2021). Chairman: Shri Partap Singh Bajwa (as listed in the official report).

## 3. Publication year [FACT]

**2021** (presented **24 March 2021**). Rajya Sabha Secretariat, New Delhi.

## 4. Scope [FACT]

Examination of the **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020** (notified 23 July 2020 under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019), including:

- Background and genesis of e-commerce consumer risks  
- Salient features of the Rules (duties of e-commerce entities, marketplace liabilities, seller duties, grievance redressal)  
- Oral evidence of the Department of Consumer Affairs  
- Observations and recommendations on data privacy, unfair trade practices, algorithms/fake reviews, drip pricing, grievance redressal, delivery charges, product information  

Official PDF: https://sansad.in/getFile/rsnew/Committee_site/Committee_File/ReportFile/12/131/245_2021_3_17.pdf?source=rajyasabha  
Access date: **2026-07-30**. [T1]

## 5. Key findings [FACT]

From the official report (selected):

- E-commerce growth creates new consumer risks (unfair trade, data, misleading practices).  
- Rules apply to e-commerce entities operating in India and those systematically offering goods/services to consumers in India.  
- Rules impose disclosure, grievance officer, and related duties; they **complement** CPA 2019.  
- Committee examined threats across information-sharing, ordering, payment, delivery, and support stages.  
- Committee emphasised **user data security**, consent (not pre-ticked checkboxes alone), and clearer definitions of unfair practices in the e-commerce context.

## 6. Relevant recommendations [FACT]

Summary of observations/recommendations in the official report (non-exhaustive; recommendations are **not statute**):

1. Categorise personal data by sensitivity; secure payment gateways.  
2. Encourage major marketplace entities to establish data centres in India; innovate models for hyperscale data centres.  
3. Link consumer personal-data protection under e-commerce rules context to the then-pending Personal Data Protection Bill framework.  
4. Clearer definition of unfair trade practices / remedies re predatory pricing and small vendors.  
5. Protect kirana / local vendors; pathways for small retailers into e-commerce.  
6. Corrective mechanisms against algorithm manipulation, fake reviews/ratings.  
7. Define drip pricing in Rules with consumer protection and penal provisions.  
8. Action against click farms distorting market signals.  
9–10. Spell out customer-care duties and multi-level grievance escalation; monitor resolution time.  
11+. Guidelines/caps themes on delivery charges; distinguish misinformation / no information / misleading information with penalties; consumer education guidelines.

**Not identified:** any recommendation that smartphone manufacturers must provide a **mandatory minimum multi-year OS upgrade or security-update period**.

## 7. Relevance to smartphone software support [ANALYSIS]

| Interface | Note |
|-----------|------|
| E-commerce product listings | Handsets sold online fall under e-commerce duties (disclosure, grievance) — not OS-support floors |
| Warranty / product information | Rules address warranty/guarantee **disclosure**; do not set multi-year software support periods |
| Misleading claims | Potential interface if OS-support claims are advertised — conduct/claims focus |
| Gap | No OEM multi-year software-support duty recommended |

## 8. Limitations

- Report examines **Rules** and recommends improvements; recommendations do not automatically amend the Rules.  
- Data-protection recommendations refer to frameworks as they stood in 2021 (pre-DPDP Act 2023 final form).  
- Pin-cites for litigation should use the official PDF page/paragraph, not this secondary note alone.
