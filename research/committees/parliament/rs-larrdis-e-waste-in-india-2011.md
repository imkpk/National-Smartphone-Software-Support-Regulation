# Parliamentary Publication Note — E-Waste in India (LARRDIS)

## 1. Report title [FACT]

**E-Waste in India** — Research Unit (LARRDIS), Rajya Sabha Secretariat, New Delhi, **June 2011**.

## 2. Committee / Commission [FACT]

**Not a Department-related Standing Committee report.**  
This is an **official occasional research paper** of the **Rajya Sabha Secretariat Research Unit (LARRDIS)** prepared for the benefit of Members of Parliament. Preface by Dr. V. K. Agnihotri, Secretary-General, Rajya Sabha.

**Why included:** official parliamentary publication materially relevant to **e-waste**, **device lifecycle**, and **electronics** regulation context. Distinct from DRSC recommendation reports.

## 3. Publication year [FACT]

**2011** (June).

## 4. Scope [FACT]

Backgrounder covering:

1. Introduction — definition, generation, global context, electronics industry growth, health/environment hazards  
2. Recycling economy — informal and formal sectors; city projections  
3. Management of e-waste — Indian regulatory regime (as of 2010–11 draft/rules era), Basel/Bamako/Rotterdam, EU WEEE/RoHS comparative notes  
4. Conclusion — EPR, imports, awareness, monitoring, waste reduction at source  

Official PDF: https://cms.rajyasabha.nic.in/UploadedFiles/ElectronicPublications/E-Waste_in_india.pdf  
Access date: **2026-07-30**. [T1]

## 5. Key findings [FACT]

From the official paper:

- E-waste is a growing environmental and public-health hazard linked to rapid electronics consumption.  
- Informal recycling economy is large; formal sector was nascent in 2011.  
- Documents then-existing hazardous-waste rules, 2008 e-waste guidelines, and **Draft E-waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 2010** discussion (pre-E-Waste Rules 2016/2022).  
- Comparative reference to **EU WEEE** producer obligations and RoHS.  
- Conclusion themes include **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**, licensed import control, producer–public–government cooperation, awareness, compliance monitoring, and **reduction of waste at source**.

## 6. Relevant recommendations [FACT]

As a research backgrounder, the paper’s “conclusion” section articulates **policy directions** (EPR, source reduction, monitoring, etc.) for parliamentary information. These are **not** House-adopted DRSC recommendations and are **not** binding law.

**Not identified:** any statement recommending a **mandatory multi-year smartphone OS/security-update support period** as Indian product law.

## 8. Limitations

- **2011** baseline; generation figures and legal regime are outdated relative to E-Waste Rules 2022.  
- Not a Standing Committee report; weight is **background research for MPs**, not committee recommendation to Government in the DRSC sense.  
- Comparative EU material is descriptive, not Indian law.
