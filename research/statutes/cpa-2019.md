# Statute Note — Consumer Protection Act, 2019

**Litigation advice:** None. Neutral statutory mapping only.

## 1. Overview [FACT]

The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** (Act No. 35 of 2019) is the principal central statute on consumer rights, unfair trade practices, misleading advertisements, product liability, and the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), replacing the 1986 framework.

**Official source:** India Code — [Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (English PDF)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/15256/1/eng201935.pdf). Access date: **2026-07-30**. [T0]

## 2. Legislative purpose [LAW/ANALYSIS]

To protect consumer interests, establish authorities for timely administration and settlement of consumer disputes, and address unfair trade practices and related matters (see long title / preamble of the Act on India Code).

## 3. Relevant provisions [LAW]

| Theme | Provisions (confirm on India Code) | Notes |
|-------|-------------------------------------|--------|
| Definitions | s. 2 | Includes “product liability” s. 2(34); “product liability action” s. 2(35); “unfair trade practice” s. 2(47); “misleading advertisement” s. 2(28) |
| Consumer rights | Chapter II concepts / Act scheme | Rights including information, safety, choice (as set out in the Act) |
| CCPA | Chapter III, ss. 10–27 (structure) | Central Authority on unfair trade practices, misleading ads, consumer rights violations as a class |
| Product liability | Chapter VI, **ss. 82–87** | Application; product liability action; manufacturer / service provider / seller liability; exceptions |
| E-commerce measures | s. 94 | Measures to prevent unfair trade practices in e-commerce, direct selling, etc. |
| Commissions | District / State / National | Individual and certain class/value complaints |

**Pin-cite discipline:** Section numbers above track India Code PDF structure as commonly published; re-verify against the India Code text before court use.

## 4. Regulatory scope [LAW]

- Goods and services supplied to consumers in trade.  
- Manufacturers, sellers, service providers, advertisers, e-commerce entities (as defined).  
- Private remedies before consumer commissions **and** regulatory action by CCPA.  
- Does **not**, on its face, create a multi-year OS-update schedule for smartphones.

## 5. Applicability to smartphone software support [ANALYSIS]

| Interface | Analytical note |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Information / UTP** | Non-disclosure or misleading claims about update support duration *may* engage unfair trade practice / misleading advertisement analysis if facts fit statutory definitions—**case-specific**. |
| **Product liability** | Harm from “defective product” under Chapter VI requires statutory elements of defect/harm; security-update cessation is **not automatically** a listed defect—OPEN for doctrinal development. |
| **CCPA** | Class-wide unfair practices / misleading ads could be investigated if evidence of systemic misrepresentation exists. |
| **Individual vs systemic** | Consumer fora address discrete grievances; they do not by themselves enact ex ante industry-wide software-support floors. |

**Not asserted:** That CPA 2019 already mandates N years of Android security updates.

## 6. Open questions [OPEN]

1. Can software-support duration be treated as a material quality representation under s. 2(47)/s. 2(28)?  
2. When does an unpatched OS constitute a “defect” for product liability?  
3. Interaction between CPA remedies and any future MeitY/BIS standards?  
4. Role of s. 94 e-commerce measures for online handset listings?
