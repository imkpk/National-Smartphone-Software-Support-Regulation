# Statute Note — Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016

## 1. Overview [FACT]

The **Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016** establishes the legal framework for the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), Indian Standards, conformity assessment, and related quality infrastructure.

**Official source:** India Code — Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016. Access date: **2026-07-30**. [T0]

## 2. Legislative purpose [LAW/ANALYSIS]

To provide for the establishment of a national standards body, development of Indian Standards, and conformity assessment to ensure quality of goods and services (see Act long title on India Code).

## 3. Relevant provisions [LAW]

| Theme | Content (confirm on India Code) | Notes |
|-------|----------------------------------|--------|
| Establishment of BIS | Early chapters of the Act | Institutional mandate |
| Indian Standards | Power to establish, publish, review standards | Technical content of standards |
| Conformity assessment / marking | Certification, Hallmarking, etc. | Market surveillance interface |
| **Compulsory use of Standard Mark / QCOs** | Provisions enabling Central Government to direct conformity to Indian Standards for goods/articles | **Quality Control Orders** pathway (often via Gazette notifications) |
| Offences / penalties | Enforcement chapters | Non-compliance consequences |

## 4. Regulatory scope [LAW]

- Goods, articles, processes, and systems as covered by standards and orders.  
- Voluntary standards can be made effectively mandatory through QCOs for specified goods.  
- No identified Indian Standard in this research pass that already mandates multi-year smartphone OS support (see negative finding / IS search note).

## 5. Applicability to smartphone software support [ANALYSIS]

| Interface | Note |
|-----------|------|
| **Standards pathway** | A future IS could address software support disclosure or minimum security-update periods; BIS Act supplies the *institutional pathway*, not the technical content. |
| **QCO pathway** | If an IS is notified as compulsory for smartphones, market entry could require compliance—**hypothetical policy design**, not current law claim. |
| **Electronics** | Existing compulsory registration / CRO-type regimes for electronics (if any) are separate notifications—verify on MeitY/BIS portals in Phase 4 policy research. |

## 6. Open questions [OPEN]

1. Existing IS numbers touching mobile phones, batteries, or safety—none identified as OS-support longevity standards.  
2. Whether software support can be framed as a “goods” quality parameter under standards law.  
3. Inter-agency role split: BIS vs MeitY vs Consumer Affairs.
