# Statute Note — Information Technology Act, 2000

## 1. Overview [FACT]

The **Information Technology Act, 2000** (as amended, including by the Information Technology (Amendment) Act, 2008) provides the principal central legal framework for electronic records, digital signatures, cyber contraventions/offences, intermediary liability themes, and the institutional basis for the **Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)**.

**Official source:** India Code — Information Technology Act, 2000. Access date: **2026-07-30**. [T0]  
Portal: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (search “Information Technology Act”).

## 2. Legislative purpose [LAW/ANALYSIS]

To provide legal recognition for electronic commerce and electronic records, and to create a framework addressing cyber wrongs and related matters (see Act preamble/long title on India Code).

## 3. Relevant provisions [LAW]

| Theme | Provisions (confirm on India Code) | Notes |
|-------|-------------------------------------|--------|
| Electronic records / signatures | Early chapters of the Act | Foundational digital legality |
| Offences / penalties | Chapters on offences (as amended) | Cybercrime enforcement—not OEM update mandates |
| Intermediaries | Safe-harbour / due diligence scheme (as amended) | Platform-facing more than handset OEMs |
| **CERT-In** | **Section 70B** (as commonly identified post-amendment) | National agency for cyber incident response; power to call for information / issue directions in its domain—confirm exact text on India Code |
| Critical systems | Related provisions on protected systems | Not product-longevity standards |

## 4. Regulatory scope [LAW]

- Electronic transactions, cyber security institutions, and cyber offences.  
- Directions and advisories under CERT-In’s statutory/administrative mandate.  
- **Does not** on its face set a minimum Android/iOS support period for consumer handsets.

## 5. Applicability to smartphone software support [ANALYSIS]

| Interface | Note |
|-----------|------|
| **CERT-In / patching culture** | National cyber posture emphasises timely response and security; public advisories often stress patching—which **presupposes** vendor patch availability. |
| **OEM vs intermediary** | Handset brand owners are not automatically “intermediaries” under the Act for OS update purposes; careful characterisation required. |
| **Gap** | Device-side multi-year support floors are a **product/standards** problem not fully specified in the IT Act text. |
| **Complement** | Consumer law, BIS standards, and environmental rules may fill different facets of the same public problem. |

## 6. Open questions [OPEN]

2. Extent of CERT-In directions to hardware vendors vs intermediaries/service providers.  
3. Interaction with Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (separate instrument—later research if tasked).
